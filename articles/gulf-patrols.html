International Naval Coalition Expands Patrols in Persian Gulf

March 13, 2026 — UBNews Defense Desk

Naval forces from multiple allied nations have increased patrol operations throughout the Persian Gulf as tensions continue to rise near the Strait of Hormuz. The expanded presence is intended to protect commercial shipping routes and monitor potential threats to merchant vessels.

Military officials say the patrols include guided-missile destroyers, surveillance aircraft, and specialized mine-countermeasure vessels capable of detecting underwater hazards. The ships are working together to maintain safe navigation through the region’s busiest shipping lanes.

Coalition patrols are designed to deter attacks on commercial vessels and provide rapid response capability if incidents occur. Naval commanders say international cooperation is critical because the waterway supports energy supplies for much of the world.

Defense analysts note that multinational patrols also allow participating countries to share intelligence and maritime surveillance data, improving situational awareness across the region.

Officials say patrol operations will likely continue as long as tensions remain elevated and shipping companies require additional security while traveling through the Gulf.
