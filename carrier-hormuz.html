US Carrier Group Moves Toward Strait of Hormuz

March 12, 2026 — UBNews Military Desk

A United States Navy carrier strike group has moved closer to the Strait of Hormuz as tensions in the region continue to rise following reports of naval mine deployments in the strategic shipping corridor.

The deployment is intended to strengthen maritime security and ensure freedom of navigation through one of the world's most critical oil transit routes. Nearly one-fifth of global oil shipments pass through the narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman.

Naval analysts say the carrier group's presence provides both surveillance and rapid-response capability should commercial shipping or allied naval forces encounter threats such as naval mines or small attack boats.

The strike group includes guided-missile destroyers, cruisers, and aircraft capable of conducting reconnaissance, strike missions, and mine detection operations across the region.

Mine countermeasure units and allied naval forces are also monitoring the situation closely while merchant shipping companies evaluate security conditions before entering the strait.

Military officials emphasized that the mission is focused on protecting international shipping lanes and maintaining stability in a region that remains a vital artery for global energy supply.
